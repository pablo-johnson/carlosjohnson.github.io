Carlos Johnson is a violinist, conductor and teacher. Born in Lima into a family of musicians, he has lived in Germany for more than three decades: he has been First Concertmaster of the Philharmonic Orchestra of the Hanseatic City of Lübeck since 2000 and Professor of Violin and Chamber Music at the Musikhochschule Lübeck since 2008.

His work moves between two traditions. On one side, the European repertoire he shapes daily from the concertmaster's desk; on the other, the Peruvian and Latin American chamber music he researches, edits, records and programmes in European halls. He calls that second task **musical decentralisation**, and he has devoted much of his career to it.

## From Lima to Europe

He began studying the violin at six, in a house where music was everyday language. In 1981, still a teenager, he made his solo debut with the National Symphony Orchestra of Peru; the following year he took first prize in the "Nueva Acrópolis" and "Joseph Haydn" competitions.

His formative years coincided with the hardest years of the Peruvian crisis, when violence and the collapse of cultural funding made a musical career unviable in the country. He emigrated at seventeen. Despite more than thirty years in Germany, his connection to Peru remains intact and is present in almost everything he programmes and records.

## Training

In 1983 a scholarship from the Organization of American States (OAS) took him to Michigan in the United States. Seeking the string school at its source, he continued in Sofia, Bulgaria, where he absorbed the discipline and carrying tone of the Eastern European tradition.

The decisive turning point came in 1985, when he entered the **Hochschule für Musik Detmold**. There he studied with Lukas David within the German tradition of *Werktreue* — fidelity to the score — and completed the **Konzertexamen**, the highest performance qualification in the German system, with distinction. On the European competition circuit he was a prizewinner at the "Nicanor Zabaleta" International Competition in San Sebastián, Spain.

A chapter of its own is his association with the Hungarian violinist and conductor **Tibor Varga**, with whose chamber orchestra in Switzerland he appeared repeatedly as guest soloist and whose personal assistant he became.

## The concertmaster's desk

His orchestral career in Germany began with six seasons in the **WDR Symphony Orchestra Cologne**, a post that demands exceptional sight-reading, a repertoire that changes weekly, and studio-grade precision.

In 2000 he won the audition for **First Concertmaster** (*Erster Konzertmeister*) of the Lübeck Philharmonic, a position he has held ever since. The role goes far beyond playing the violin solos in a symphonic score: the concertmaster unifies bowings, fingerings and articulation across the entire string section, and translates the conductor's gesture into concrete technical instructions.

German critics have described his sound as a "light, lyrical ascent" and praised him as "singing and assured on the violin", leading passages such as the *Abendsegen* from Humperdinck's *Hänsel und Gretel* or Mendelssohn's "Scottish" Symphony with taste and atmosphere.

## Soloist and chamber musician

As a soloist he has performed with ensembles including the Sinfonieorchester Berlin and the Detmolder Kammerorchester, and his tours have taken him across Europe, Asia and the Americas, with appearances in Japan, South Korea, Chile and the United States, alongside recordings for labels and international broadcasters.

Chamber music is his laboratory. He founded the **Lübeck Chamber Orchestra**, where he is first violin, and has founded or been a member of the Bachus Quartett, the Cuarteto Pentagrama, the Cuarteto Prentki, the Trío Tre Mondi and the Trio Arpeggione. At the Theater Lübeck he leads demanding projects, among them a cycle devoted to the Second Viennese School: Berg, Schoenberg and Webern.

In Peru he has taken part repeatedly in the "Lukas David" International Chamber Music Festivals in Lima, named after his own teacher in Detmold.

## The podium

In 2017 he formally began his career as a conductor, studying with the German conductor **Ludwig Pflanz**. Since then he has been invited to conduct symphonic ensembles in Europe and the Americas.

In May 2024 he took on the double role of conductor and solo violinist with the **National Symphony Orchestra of Colombia**, in the *play/direct* format, leading from the instrument. The Universidad Nacional de Música in Peru issued a public commendation for that performance.

He has also brought his teaching instinct to the podium with the *Klangbilderkonzerte*, concerts staged with the Lübeck state museums that cross literature and music — one of them wove Günter Grass's novella *Im Krebsgang* together with works by Bach and Ravel.

## Research and discography

> "Written music has no value whatsoever if it is not played."

Against the widespread assumption that barely two or three Peruvian composers had written for violin and piano, Johnson began a search that lasted years, through archives and libraries. It turned up numerous manuscript and unpublished works, many of them forgotten on the shelves of the Universidad Nacional de Música or held in private collections.

With the pianist **Katia Palacios Luna** he recorded the first two volumes of the collection **Compositores Peruanos** (2018), made at the UNM under the direction of the musicologist Omar Ponce Valdivia and now one of the founding projects of the CICREMP research centre:

- **Volume 1** — Manuel Aguirre, Federico Gerdes, Carlos Valderrama, Renzo Bracesco, Ernesto López Mindreau, Alberto Díaz Robles and Andrés Sas.
- **Volume 2** — Teodoro Valcárcel, Alfonso de Silva, Armando Guevara Ochoa and Roberto Carpio.

At the opposite end of the repertoire, in 2013 he was the principal performer — alongside the cellist Hans-Christian Schwarz — of **volume 17 of the Friedhelm Döhl Edition**, released by the German label Dreyer Gaido, with works of the German post-war avant-garde such as *Gesang der Frühe*, *Sphinx* and *Revelge*.

## Musical decentralisation

In 2010 the then President of Germany, Horst Köhler, presented him with an official award for his work as an ambassador for South American music in Germany and abroad.

Johnson often contrasts the roughly 130 professional symphony orchestras sustained by the German state with the four official ensembles that exist in Peru, and he has publicly named budget cuts such as the one that hit the Arequipa Symphony Orchestra. His answer to that imbalance runs in two directions: bringing Peruvian and Latin American composers into European halls, and bringing young Latin American musicians closer to the standards of international institutions.

The clearest example is **"Musik aus Lateinamerika"**, the concert he programmed for the Theater Lübeck chamber series in December 2024 with the pianist Bertan Balli. Alongside names already familiar to German audiences — Piazzolla, Villa-Lobos, Ginastera — he included the Peruvians José Carlos Campos, Ernesto López Mindreau and Sadiel Cuentas, the Mexicans Carlos Chávez and Silvestre Revueltas, and the Venezuelan Paul Desenne. The evening also featured the world premiere of a work written for him by the Uruguayan bandoneonist and composer **Luis Di Matteo**.

From the stage he explained to the European audience the syncretism that underpins Latin American concert music: European harmonic architecture on one side and, on the other, the Andean pentatonic scale inherited from the Inca world, which still colours the region's contemporary music.

## On interpretation

> "I don't play my own music. I play music written by other people. That is why I believe it is right to respect the inspiration and the ideas of those people, and to try to understand them."
