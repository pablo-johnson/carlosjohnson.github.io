The violin is the centre from which everything else follows. Carlos Johnson has played it since he was six — and since 2000 he has done so from the most demanding position a violinist can hold inside a German orchestra.

## The concertmaster's desk

His orchestral career in Germany began with six seasons in the **WDR Symphony Orchestra Cologne**. A radio orchestra is a school of its own: repertoire that changes every week, a great deal of contemporary music in first performance, and the precision a recording studio demands, where the microphone records every impurity.

In 2000 he won the audition for **First Concertmaster** (*Erster Konzertmeister*) of the Philharmonic Orchestra of the Hanseatic City of Lübeck, a position he has held ever since.

The role goes far beyond playing the violin solos written into the score. The concertmaster unifies bowings, fingerings and articulation across the whole string section — more than half the orchestra — and translates the conductor's gesture into concrete technical instruction. In practice he is the bridge between the podium and the desks.

German critics have described his sound as a *"light, lyrical ascent"* and praised him as *"singing and assured on the violin"* when leading passages such as the *Abendsegen* from Humperdinck's *Hänsel und Gretel* or Mendelssohn's "Scottish" Symphony with taste and atmosphere.

## Soloist

Alongside the orchestral work he has sustained an international solo career, with ensembles including the Sinfonieorchester Berlin, the Detmolder Kammerorchester and the Kammerorchester Tibor Varga in Switzerland, where he appeared repeatedly at Varga's own invitation.

His tours have taken him across Europe, Asia and the Americas, with performances in Japan, South Korea, Chile and the United States, alongside recordings for labels and international broadcasters.

## Chamber music

If the orchestra is the craft, chamber music is the laboratory. In a quartet or a trio the violinist is exposed: there is no section to lean on, and dialogue, balance and intonation are settled note by note.

He founded the **Lübeck Chamber Orchestra**, where he is first violin, and has founded or played in the Bachus Quartett, the Cuarteto Pentagrama, the Cuarteto Prentki, the Trío Tre Mondi and the Trio Arpeggione.

At the Theater Lübeck he leads demanding programmes, among them a cycle devoted to the Second Viennese School — Berg, Schoenberg and Webern — a repertoire that requires understanding atonality and twelve-tone writing from the inside.

In Peru he has taken part repeatedly in the "Lukas David" International Chamber Music Festivals in Lima, named after his teacher in Detmold.

## Repertoire

His reputation was built on the European canon — Mozart, Bach, Schubert, Schumann, Mendelssohn, Dvořák — but it does not end there. To it he adds the Peruvian and Latin American chamber music he has recovered and recorded, and the German post-war avant-garde, with works by Friedhelm Döhl that push the instrument to its physical limits.

Few violinists move with equal authority between the symphonic indigenismo of Armando Guevara Ochoa and the atonality of Döhl.
